MEP engineering excellence

Three disciplines. One integrated model.

Minimising errors means catching them in the calculation, not the commissioning script. We author the designs, check them independently, and stay on site while they are installed.

Power train, source to rack.

From utility intake through HV/LV distribution, generators and UPS to busway and rack PDU — modelled, calculated and checked against the resilience concept the client actually sold to their tenant.

  • Load flow, short-circuit and protection coordination studies
  • Arc-flash assessment and labelling strategy
  • Generator sizing, step-load acceptance and fuel autonomy
  • UPS topology, battery autonomy and selective tripping

Typical design envelope

  • Topology2N · N+1 · block redundant
  • Distribution11 / 33 kV intake → 400 V
  • Rack density8 – 40 kW per rack
  • UPS autonomy5 – 10 min at full load
  • Generator start< 10 s to on-load

Heat out, uptime in.

Cooling is where density bites. We model airflow and hydraulics together so the white space performs at design day and at 20% load — the condition most halls actually run at.

  • CFD modelling of white space and containment strategy
  • Chilled-water hydraulics, pressure drop and pump selection
  • Chiller, CRAH and free-cooling hours analysis
  • Liquid-cooling readiness for high-density halls

Typical design envelope

  • Supply air22 – 27 °C (ASHRAE A1)
  • Chilled water18 / 24 °C flow / return
  • Design PUE1.20 – 1.35 annualised
  • Ride-through60 – 120 s stored volume
  • ContainmentHot aisle, blanked

The layer that proves the other two work.

Controls are the most common cause of a failed integrated systems test. We review sequences of operation line by line and write the scripts that prove them before witness day.

  • Sequence of operation authoring and peer review
  • BMS / EPMS points list and alarm rationalisation
  • Failure-mode scripting for integrated systems testing
  • Handover data, O&M structure and operator training

DfMA

Why we push work off-site.

Fabrication and site preparation run simultaneously. That single change compresses the critical path, and everything downstream of it gets more predictable.

Faster deployment

Parallel fabrication and site prep collapse the overall programme duration.

Enhanced quality

A controlled factory environment delivers consistent, repeatable precision.

Improved safety

Fewer on-site activities means less congestion and lower exposure at height.

Cost efficiency

Predictable schedules and reduced waste produce accurate, defensible costs.

Optimised logistics

Just-in-time delivery removes on-site storage and laydown disruption.

Scalable solutions

Modular skids and pods make expansion and future upgrades straightforward.
